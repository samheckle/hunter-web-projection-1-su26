## Project Questions?

Frequently asked questions:
- Why isn't my site live?
	1. Check your URL. Is the URL in your URL bar the correct one?
	2. Check your folders. Did you link the correct folder in the URL?
	3. Did you wait enough time for the site to be published?
		- GitHub pages takes ~5 minutes to propagate and your sites will not be immediately published. 
		- You can check the status by going to your repository → Actions → Check the most recent deployment. 
		- If it is green, the site is live. If it is brown, the site is still being published.
- Why aren't my images showing up?
	1. Check your url of your pictures. When uploading, they always need the *relative path*. So it should be a short URL.

## Critique Ground Rules

Good critique is foundational in pushing a project further. The way we will be doing critique in this class will be both verbal and written.

At the end of each student presentation, I will ask if there is anything specific that you had feelings about: any uncertainty, technical or conceptual implementation, or things that you need an extra set of eyes and brains on. 

### Giving Criticism

Typically critique works best when mentioning *specifics*. To guide you towards specificity, you can follow this interpreted outline from *Varieties of Visual Experience* by Edmund Burke:
1. Description: What are you seeing?
2. Analysis: How is it made? What does it make you think about or feel? What does it remind you of?
3. Interpretation: What is it about? What is the main idea being explored?
4. Evaluation: Is the project successful in communicating ideas? Does it explore the prompt in compelling, interesting, or unique ways?

**Avoid "I like it"**: This is not constructive or useful critique. You can be *specific* by picking up on details, observing them and comment on why you noticed it or why it stood out to you.

Critique will be in three parts:
- During the presentations, you will take notes using your tool of choice.
- After the presentation, you will write in chat or unmute yourself and provide some critique of the project. Everyone will need to participate in some way.
- For the last portion of the class, you will write up your critique as a reply to the discussion post. 

### Receiving Criticism

Understanding yourself is key to developing the skill to receive criticism. Critical feedback is not negative, but bringing together several minds to push each project further. Understanding how to separate yourself as an individual vs. the identity of your work is an important skill!

Generally, receiving criticism opens the door for you to explore your works more deeply. When receiving criticism, know that everyone is coming from unique experiences and may understand your project differently than yourself. 

Ways in which you can receive criticism, adapted from *The Artist's Way* by Julia Cameron:
- Jot down notes for yourself what concepts are compelling or bother you
- Remember even if you made bad art, it may be a necessary stepping stone to your next work
- Has this criticism been mentioned before in previous works? See if there are patterns you need to re-examine.
- Creativity is the only cure for criticism. Build something new or build on top of the criticism.
